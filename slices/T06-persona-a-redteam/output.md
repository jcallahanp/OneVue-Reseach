# T06 Persona A Red-Team Inventory (software-only, one machine)

Scope: DEFENSIVE threat inventory for the certification owner. Persona A = advanced candidate, multiple home computers, willing to prepare their own machine, using **NO external hardware, NO confederate, NO proxy taker, NO second physical device**. Attacker goal: run OnVUE and consult an LLM / answer bank on the **same single physical machine**, undetected. Defended deployment: 360° pre-exam room/tech check, then during the exam **NO screen monitoring and NO monitoring of the candidate's computer** — only camera video, mic audio, and OnVUE's own client telemetry/biometrics (T05 §10). Mechanism/category level only; this is not an operational tutorial. All access dates **2026-11-12**.

Analytical frame (from T05): with the screen unobserved, exactly **two barriers** stand in the way — **(1)** OnVUE's client-side host-integrity checks (process scan §3, VM detection §4–5, single-display §6, remote-control/screen-share block §7, app-switch/focus lockdown §2/§8), and **(2)** the candidate still having to **VIEW** answers somewhere. Barrier (2) is unusually cheap here: because nothing observes the screen, answer pixels on the candidate's own display are seen by no one, and keeping them on the **primary** display keeps the candidate's gaze natural on camera. So the real contest is almost entirely Barrier (1), plus the two always-on sensors the screen-blindness does **not** relax: the **microphone** (kills audio delivery) and the **camera/biometrics** (kills looking-away and liveness fraud).

## Executive answer to Persona A (which 1–3 strategy families are the real answer, and why)

**Family C/B — "No-VM, in-the-blind-spot local tooling" is the real answer.** A fully local, offline answer engine (local LLM + indexed cert bank) that **reads the exam** through an accessibility/UI-automation channel or local OCR, and **surfaces answers on the same primary display** via a non-activating always-on-top overlay, all **staged into memory before the secure client launches**. Why it is strongest for Persona A:
- It **never touches virtualization**, so it entirely sidesteps OnVUE's *only documented hard-BLOCK* host-integrity control — VM detection (T05 §4). That control is the arms-race Persona A most wants to avoid.
- It needs **no network**, so the telemetry/stream hosts (`*.onvue.com`, `*.proctorcam.com`, `*.wowza.com`, `*.ably.io`, `*.twilio.com`; T05 §10) see nothing anomalous in content terms.
- Answers stay on the **primary screen**, so gaze stays on-monitor — lowest camera/biometric risk (T05 §10). No audio, so the mic (the one always-on screen-blind-spot-defeating sensor) has nothing to catch.
- It lives in OnVUE's three biggest blind spots: **screen unobserved** (§10), **process-scan internals undocumented** (§3, "enumeration method not documented"), and **foreground-only lockdown** (§2/§8 polices app-switch *keys* and focus, not passive non-activating windows).
- **Binding constraints** (where a defender should concentrate): (a) the helper surviving the process scan, and (b) the overlay surviving OnVUE's window z-order / fullscreen mode. If `proctorapp` runs exclusive-fullscreen or owns top z-order, the overlay fails and the read channel must substitute an in-field surface (IME/suggestion) or the candidate falls back to Family A.

**Secondary — Family A/E — "Stealthed Type-1 hypervisor + GPU passthrough, OnVUE as hardened guest."** The cleanest *isolation* (the helper lives outside the guest and the OnVUE client cannot enumerate across the VM boundary), but gated by the VM-detection arms race. Signature-based VM tells (CPUID hypervisor bit, paravirt drivers, SMBIOS/MAC strings, the "virtual machine detected" trigger set, T05 §4–5) are defeatable by a well-prepared Persona A; **timing-based hypervisor detection remains the hard, unsolved part** (CPUID VM-exit latency, RDTSC smoothing — academically treated as an open problem). Medium feasibility for a determined Persona A with a dedicated prepared machine; the highest-skill option.

**What is NOT the answer (closed paths):** casual VM/WSL/Hyper-V helpers (OnVUE over-blocks these, T05 §5); any **audio** delivery (mic, T05 §10); **loopback remote-desktop** to a hidden session (remote-control/screen-share category block, T05 §7); and anything needing a **second display or device** (out of scope, and multi-display is flagged, T05 §6).

## Detailed entries

Each: **mechanism (2–4 sentences) | control defeated (T05) | residual risk | platform | feasibility | one detection signal.** Mechanism stays at the category level.

**1. Non-activating on-screen overlay (B/D).** A topmost, layered, no-activate, click-through window renders answer text directly over the exam on the same primary display; because it never takes foreground focus and never intercepts input, it triggers no focus-loss or app-switch event. Community/vendor evidence confirms topmost + click-through + transparent is achievable over **borderless/windowed** apps but **not over exclusive-fullscreen**, where the compositor is bypassed [overlay refs]. The candidate reads normally with gaze on the monitor. *Control defeated:* focus/app-switch lockdown (§2/§8) and the screen-unobserved gap (§10). *Residual:* if `proctorapp` runs exclusive-fullscreen or asserts top z-order/own-draw, the overlay is covered; the helper process can be caught by the §3 scan. *Platform:* Windows strongest; macOS via high `NSWindow` level + non-activating collection behavior. *Feasibility:* **High** (conditioned on windowed/borderless exam). *Signal:* enumerate layered/topmost windows above the exam surface and flag any non-exam `WS_EX_LAYERED` topmost window / elevated-level NSWindow.

**2. Fully local offline LLM + indexed answer bank (C).** A quantized local model (llama.cpp / Ollama class) plus a vector index of the certification's material runs entirely on-device, so no exam-time network traffic carries question/answer content and the telemetry hosts see a normal stream. Offline local vision+text models for screenshot/question answering are now routine [local-LLM refs]. Answers are produced locally and routed to an on-screen surface (entry 1) or read channel (entry 5). *Control defeated:* content-level network monitoring (none exists) and VM detection (§4 — there is no VM). *Residual:* the model runtime is a process the §3 scan might catch; sustained CPU/GPU and fan/thermal load during an otherwise light exam is a host-telemetry/acoustic tell. *Platform:* both. *Feasibility:* **High.** *Signal:* host-load / GPU-utilization anomaly via client telemetry, plus continuous local-process baseline diffing.

**3. Pre-exam staging / memory-resident helper (G).** All helper components are launched, hidden, and made memory-resident **before** `proctorapp` starts and before the room/tech scan, so the point-in-time "close all applications" gate and the visual scan find a clean machine (T05 §1/§3). They surface only once the exam is underway. *Control defeated:* the pre-exam close-down gate, which is a snapshot, not a continuous guarantee. *Residual:* T05 §1 notes OnVUE "shuts down prohibited processes it detects" during check-in and may re-scan during the exam; durable hiding/persistence is the hard part. *Platform:* both. *Feasibility:* **High** against a snapshot gate. *Signal:* continuous (not check-in-only) process scanning with a baseline diff of everything spawned before client launch.

**4. Passive foreground-preservation (D).** Every helper interaction is designed to avoid foregrounding: rendering via non-activating windows, reading via background APIs, control via a background process — so `GetForegroundWindow`/`WM_KILLFOCUS`-style signals never change and the exam window keeps focus throughout. T05 §2/§8 shows the lockdown blocks app-switch **keys** and flags focus loss; a helper that never activates produces neither. *Control defeated:* focus-loss flagging. *Residual:* low-level input hooks (for hotkeys) or z-order polling could expose the helper even without a focus change. *Platform:* both. *Feasibility:* **High.** *Signal:* detect global input hooks and any non-exam window still receiving paint/compose cycles.

**5. Accessibility / UI-Automation read channel (C).** Windows UI Automation (`TextPattern`) and macOS Accessibility (`AXUIElement`, `AXSelectedText`/`AXFocusedWindow`) can read another app's exposed UI text directly from the tree — the same channel screen readers use — feeding exam text to the local agent with no screen capture and no focus change [UIA/AX refs]. T05 §8 confirms a11y software is permitted (with accommodation), implying a usable a11y carve-out and that the APIs function against the client. *Control defeated:* avoids §3/§8 capture blocks and any focus change; reading is passive. *Residual:* if the exam renders in a canvas/custom control that exposes no text, this degrades to OCR (entry 6); macOS requires user-granted Accessibility (and, on recent macOS, Screen-Recording) permission, which is visible in Privacy settings. *Platform:* Windows (UIA), macOS (AX). *Feasibility:* **Med-High.** *Signal:* flag an unexpected UIA/AX client attaching to `proctorapp` (`UiaClientsAreListening`, `AXObserver` attachment) as assistive-tech-without-accommodation.

**6. Local OCR of the exam framebuffer + local LLM (C).** A background agent captures the exam region via desktop-duplication (DXGI on Windows, `CGDisplayStream` on macOS) or window blit, OCRs it (Tesseract or a local vision model), and answers locally. Because the screen is unobserved (§10), the capture is never streamed anywhere; the bet is that a renamed/benign agent evades **signature-based** blocking of "screen capture tools." *Control defeated:* §3 process scan if the agent matches no known-tool signature; the screen-unobserved gap. *Residual:* **highest of the C family** — desktop-duplication/capture is exactly the category T05 §3/§8 says Browser Lock blocks; OnVUE may hook or deny duplication APIs. *Platform:* both. *Feasibility:* **Med.** *Signal:* monitor desktop-duplication/capture API usage against the exam window by non-exam processes.

**7. Stealthed Type-1 hypervisor; OnVUE as hardened guest (A/E).** A thin bare-metal hypervisor boots first; OnVUE runs in a guest hardened to mask hypervisor presence (CPUID hypervisor-leaf handling, RDTSC/timing smoothing, removal of paravirt device/driver signatures, SMBIOS/ACPI/MAC spoofing to OEM values), while the answer tool lives in the host partition or a sibling guest the OnVUE client cannot enumerate across the VM boundary. Signature-based VM tells are defeatable, but the literature treats **timing-based** detection (CPUID VM-exit latency ≈ native ~300 vs VM ~thousands of cycles; prime+probe; implicit-exit timing) as the hardest to fully conceal and an open problem [VM-evasion refs]. *Control defeated:* §3 process scan (helper invisible across the boundary); clean isolation. *Residual:* OnVUE's VM detection (§4–5) is the one documented hard-BLOCK and may use timing or driver/device enumeration; single-display (§6) still applies inside the guest; leftover virtual-NIC OUI / disk vendor strings. *Platform:* Windows guest (a macOS guest is harder/licence-constrained). *Feasibility:* **Med** (prepared Persona A) / Low casual. *Signal:* add a timing red-pill probe (CPUID/RDTSC VM-exit latency vs a bare-metal baseline) plus ACPI/SMBIOS OEM-coherence checks, rather than relying on string/driver signatures.

**8. GPU/device passthrough for near-native guest presentation (E).** Passthrough (DDA on Windows hosts, VFIO on Linux hosts) gives the guest a real GPU so graphics, WebGL, and render timing look native, combined with SMBIOS/serial/MAC spoofing to bare-metal values and suppression of guest-additions/balloon devices. This targets OnVUE's **signature** heuristics for "looks like a VM." *Control defeated:* VM signature checks (§4). *Residual:* timing side channels persist; absence of coherent physical-sensor data (battery discharge curve, thermal/ambient) is an attestation tell; the hypervisor CPUID bit if not masked. *Platform:* Windows (DDA) / Linux host (VFIO). *Feasibility:* **Med.** *Signal:* cross-check hardware-attestation (TPM-measured boot) and device-sensor coherence that a passthrough guest struggles to forge.

**9. Fast user switching / second concurrent session (B).** Windows (and macOS) support multiple simultaneous logged-in sessions; the helper runs in a second user session or a Session-0 service while OnVUE occupies the interactive session. If OnVUE's process scan enumerates only the current session (undocumented, §3), cross-session helper processes may be outside its view; the candidate never actually switches sessions (that would lose focus) and the helper feeds answers via overlay/IPC. *Control defeated:* assumed single-session scope of §3. *Residual:* the client may enumerate all sessions; a second interactive session is itself an artifact. *Platform:* Windows (macOS fast-user-switching analogous). *Feasibility:* **Med.** *Signal:* enumerate processes across all sessions and Session-0 services; flag any secondary interactive session during the exam.

**10. Virtual display adapter as off-camera render/compositing surface (E).** A Windows Indirect Display Driver (IddCx) creates a user-mode virtual monitor in Session 0 with a custom EDID [IDD refs]; the agent can render its working UI to that virtual head and composite only final answers onto the real display, keeping its scratch surface "off-camera." *Control defeated:* the single-display check (§6) **if** OnVUE counts only physically-attached outputs. *Residual:* a virtual head typically **adds** an enumerated display, which risks the very multi-monitor flag it hopes to dodge (§6). *Platform:* Windows (IDD); macOS has no clean equivalent. *Feasibility:* **Med.** *Signal:* enumerate **all** graphics adapters and monitors including indirect/virtual (IddCx) and non-PnP, not just physically-attached panels.

**11. macOS platform asymmetry (F).** macOS enforcement is bounded by user-granted TCC permissions (Camera, Microphone, Automation, Input Monitoring; T05 §2) and the sandbox limits on what `proctorapp` may learn about other apps; a candidate can scope/withhold grants, or exploit full-screen-app-as-own-Space separation and Mission Control behavior [macOS Spaces refs]. The lockdown can only police what macOS exposes to it. *Control defeated:* whatever the OS withholds from the client (§2/§11). *Residual:* OnVUE may refuse to launch without the full grant set; the macOS-14+ floor (§11). *Platform:* macOS. *Feasibility:* **Med.** *Signal:* require and verify the complete permission set at launch and continuously; enumerate Spaces/Mission-Control state; run parity tests of macOS vs Windows controls so the weaker platform is not a soft target.

**12. Fully local answer-injection agent (C).** The agent reads the exam (entry 5/6), computes answers locally, and optionally selects them via synthetic input (`SendInput`/CGEvent) — a purely local action, not a network remote-control session. T05 §7 shows remote-control detection is oriented at remote tools/protocols, which a same-machine input agent is not. *Control defeated:* remote-control/screen-share detection (§7) does not model local synthetic input. *Residual:* injected input often has inhuman cadence/precision visible in answer-timing telemetry; sending into a secure window may need UIAccess privilege. Display-only (human clicks) removes most of this risk. *Platform:* both. *Feasibility:* **Med** (display-only **High**). *Signal:* input-cadence biometrics on answer selection plus an injected-input flag (`LLKHF_INJECTED`).

**13. VBS/HVCI / nested-virtualization ambiguity (A/D).** Windows VBS/HVCI themselves run a hypervisor under the OS, and OnVUE's handling of these states is undocumented (T05 §5 UNKNOWN). A candidate could probe the exact hypervisor/VBS feature-set OnVUE tolerates and hide a helper inside a tolerated nested configuration, or exploit a false-positive/allow boundary. Microsoft docs show nested virtualization and VBS historically conflict and must be configured deliberately [nested-virt refs]. *Control defeated:* the undocumented hypervisor-state handling (§5). *Residual:* T05 §5 shows OnVUE **over-blocks** WSL/Hyper-V/VBS surfaces, so this is fragile and may simply fail closed. *Platform:* Windows. *Feasibility:* **Low-Med.** *Signal:* set an explicit policy and read the hypervisor-present flag; enumerate `vmwp`/`vmcompute` workers and nested-virt capability rather than guessing from a string.

**14. Secure-client subversion (D).** Browser Lock is a user-mode app (`proctorapp`) with no vendor-documented admin requirement (T05 §2); a same-or-higher-privilege helper can, in principle, hook/patch the client's own enumeration and window-management calls so the client reports a clean host while the helper runs. This attacks the client's observations rather than hiding from them. *Control defeated:* potentially all client-side checks at once (§2–8), since the client's own view is falsified. *Residual:* code-signing/self-checksums/anti-hook, EDR flagging the injection, and any server-side attestation; this is a reverse-engineering arms race. *Platform:* both. *Feasibility:* **Low-Med.** *Signal:* client self-integrity and anti-injection verification (no foreign modules/hooks in `proctorapp`) backed by server-side binary attestation.

**15. OS Picture-in-Picture answer surface (B).** OS/browser Picture-in-Picture floats a small always-on-top, non-activating window that can survive some fullscreen states and display a pre-rendered or live-generated answer feed. *Control defeated:* focus lockdown (§2/§8), as PiP does not activate. *Residual:* PiP is video-oriented and needs a content pipeline; the client may enumerate the PiP window. *Platform:* both. *Feasibility:* **Low-Med.** *Signal:* flag any system PiP window present during the exam.

**16. Virtual camera loop to mask behavior (camera).** Although the screen is unobserved, the camera and gaze/room are observed; a software virtual camera could substitute a benign "candidate looking at screen" feed. T05 §9 marks software virtual-cam detection UNKNOWN and cites analog research where an OBS virtual cam went undetected. *Control defeated:* the (unverified) virtual-camera detection path (§9). *Residual:* facial-comparison biometrics, random liveness prompts, and head-pose analytics (§10) can break a static/looped feed; the mic remains live. *Platform:* both. *Feasibility:* **Low-Med** (biometric-risky). *Signal:* random liveness challenges plus virtual-camera driver/device-path detection and frame-authenticity analytics.

**17. Separate virtual desktop / Space switched to (B) — largely defeated.** Hosting the helper on another Windows Virtual Desktop or macOS Space and switching to it requires an app-switch/focus action that the lockdown blocks or flags (§2/§8); on macOS a full-screen exam occupies its own Space and leaving it is an app-switch event. Included to show this is a path the browser-lock **does** close. *Control defeated:* none reliably. *Residual:* the switch is the violation. *Platform:* both. *Feasibility:* **Low.** *Signal:* virtual-desktop/Space-change event hook (largely redundant with focus/app-switch detection).

**18. Preloaded IME / autocomplete dictionary (C).** For fill-in-the-blank items, a custom input method or autocomplete dictionary preloaded with answer snippets surfaces text as "suggestions" inside the exam's own fields; an IME is an OS text service, not an application the close-all gate removes. *Control defeated:* §3 process scan (text service is not an app). *Residual:* only works for text-entry items; IME switching may be blocked; narrow value. *Platform:* both. *Feasibility:* **Low.** *Signal:* lock input-method/text services to a known-good set during the exam.

**19. Embedded-webview inspection / client-side answer leakage (D).** If `proctorapp` embeds a Chromium webview, a same-privilege process might attach to a remote-debugging port or inspect the DOM; this only yields answers if any item content/keys are present client-side. *Control defeated:* none directly; depends on client architecture. *Residual:* scoring is normally server-side and debug ports are disabled. *Platform:* both. *Feasibility:* **Low.** *Signal:* disable webview remote-debugging, keep scoring server-side, and detect debugger attachment.

**20. Audio (TTS) answer delivery (C) — defeated by the mic.** A local agent could speak answers aloud, but the always-on microphone is the one sensor the screen-blindness does not relax, and "talking/mumbling" is a named violation (T05 §10). (An earpiece would be hardware — out of scope.) *Control defeated:* none; this is closed by §10. *Residual:* the mic catches it. *Platform:* both. *Feasibility:* **Low.** *Signal:* system-audio-activity plus voice/TTS detection on the mic stream.

## What OnVUE would NOT detect at all vs what it still catches

**Would NOT detect (the exploitable blind spots):**
- **On-screen answer content** — nothing captures or streams the screen (T05 §10); answer pixels on the candidate's own display are seen by no one.
- **A passive local LLM doing inference** — no exam-time network content, so the telemetry/stream hosts (§10) have nothing anomalous to carry.
- **A non-activating overlay that never steals focus** — the lockdown models app-switch keys and focus loss (§2/§8), not a topmost no-activate window (provided it beats z-order/fullscreen mode).
- **Passive UIA/AX reads** — unless OnVUE specifically watches for assistive-tech clients attaching (§8 implies a11y is tolerated).
- **A pre-staged, memory-resident helper** — if the process scan is point-in-time at check-in rather than continuous (§1/§3).
- **Software virtual cameras / virtual audio devices** — detection UNKNOWN/unverified (§9); analog research suggests some proctoring stacks miss them.
- **VBS/HVCI / nested-virtualization edge states** — handling undocumented (§5).

**Still catches (the hard barriers):**
- **VM signatures** — "a virtual machine has been detected" hard-BLOCK via CPUID/driver/device/error-string triggers (§4); the one documented, decisive host-integrity control.
- **Running remote-control / screen-share / known screen-capture tools** — category block (§3/§7/§8), including loopback RDP/VNC.
- **Multiple active displays** — required single display; flagged/terminated, and a virtual head is at risk of being counted (§6).
- **Audio** — talking/sound via the always-on mic (§10).
- **Gaze, room, and third parties** — camera + automated video analytics + facial-comparison biometrics (§10); looking away or a second person is flagged.
- **Genuine app-switching / focus loss** — blocked app-switch hotkeys and focus-loss flagging (§2/§8).
- **Casual virtualization surfaces** — WSL/Hyper-V/VirtualMachinePlatform/Docker often trip the VM false-positive even in the background (§5), which paradoxically closes the easy same-machine-VM helper route and forces either Type-1 stealth or no-VM local tooling.

**Defender's leverage summary:** the single highest-value additions are (1) a **timing-based hypervisor probe** (closes the Type-1/passthrough family where signatures fail), (2) **continuous, all-session process + API-use scanning** (closes pre-staging, second-session, and local-capture families), (3) **a top-of-z-order window/overlay audit** (closes the strongest, highest-feasibility overlay family), and (4) **assistive-tech-client attach detection** plus **host-load telemetry** (closes the passive read + local-LLM families). The microphone and camera/biometrics already close audio delivery and gaze-based fraud; the screen blind spot is what the rest of the inventory exploits.
